# Structural escapes, not better interpolating functions, can beat Cassini

No  peer-reviewed theory in  the MOND vein  has  a  published calculation showing that it passes both the Cassini external-field quadrupole, **Q2 = (1.6 ± 1.8)×10⁻²⁷ s⁻²** ([Park et al. 2026, PRD 114, 024066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17884)), and the SPARC radial acceleration relation. The tension the program found is real and published: 8.7σ in [Desmond, Hees & Famaey 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04796) and 3–15σ in Park et al. 2026, who show that reshaping the interpolating function (IF) cannot remove it because **Q2 depends only on the IF near the Sun's Galactic field g_e ≈ 2a0**. The candidates fall into three groups. Modified inertia (MI), superfluid dark matter (SfDM) under its "bubble" argument, baryon-interacting DM and MOG pass Cassini by construction, because Q2 is not a MOND observable in them, and each pays for that pass elsewhere: no relativistic theory, failure on weak lensing, no external-field effect (EFE), or abandoning MOND. Generalized QUMOND (GQUMOND), AeST in its two-field regime or at finite m_×, TRIMOND, Galileon k-mouflage, Khronon and nonlocal MOND have Solar-System limits that are not single-function AQUAL/QUMOND, but nobody has computed their Q2, so this is where new work can matter. Standard BIMOND, νHDM, dipolar DM, original TeVeS, generalized Einstein-aether, Verlinde's emergent gravity and quantised inertia either inherit the AQUAL/QUMOND tension or are dead for other reasons. For a program whose QUMOND scans found no passing IF, the best raw material is the theories that make the Solar-System IF and the galaxy IF different functions by structure: first GQUMOND, then AeST in its two-field regime, then modified inertia with Milgrom's mixed MG–MI family, then TRIMOND.

## A universal interpolating function cannot satisfy both data sets

The QUMOND quadrupole comes from the Poisson-like (elliptic) structure of the field equation. Phantom matter near the Sun–Galaxy balance point, at the MOND radius R_M = (GM/a0)^{1/2} ≈ 8×10³ au, produces a tidal-like anomaly in the inner Solar System. Milgrom showed that it persists "even if the MOND interpolating function is arbitrarily close to unity at high accelerations", and that it is "not generic to the MOND paradigm, but depend[s] on the particular MOND formulation" ([Milgrom 2009, MNRAS 399, 474](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.4817)). Blanchet & Novak's numerical solutions found that only IFs with "a very rapid transition" survive the planetary perihelion bounds ([MNRAS 412, 2530](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.1349)). The first Cassini bound, **(3 ± 3)×10⁻²⁷ s⁻²**, then excluded μ1,2,3, μexp and the original TeVeS function ([Hees et al. 2014, PRD 89, 102002](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.6950)).

The  galaxy side pulls  the other way.  SPARC  fixes  **g† = 1.20 ± 0.02 (rnd) ± 0.24 (sys) ×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻²** ([Lelli et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08981)), with 0.057 dex rms in fits to individual galaxies and no credible variation of a0 between galaxies ([Li et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00022)), and it wants a *smoother* transition than Cassini allows. With the 2026 Q2, Park et al.'s Table II gives the tension for each choice of mass-to-light (M/L) prior:

| M/L prior | Tension (no EFE / AQUAL EFE) |
|---|---|
| Fiducial SPARC | **15.0σ / 15.2σ** |
| Free hyperpriors | 12.0σ / 11.9σ |
| Constrained uniform | 15.3σ / 15.5σ |
| No-bulge | **3.8σ / 2.8σ** |
| Only-bulge | **15.9σ / 15.9σ** |

Source: [arXiv:2602.17884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17884).

The  tension is carried  by **bulge-hosting galaxies**,  which  dominate  the 10⁻¹⁰ to 10⁻⁹ m s⁻² transition region. Park et al. add a model-independent corollary. Q2 limits the MOND boost at the Sun's position to **< 1.01–1.018 (95%)**, while the Milky Way rotation curve requires at least about 10%. That is an internal tension of **about 11σ** for any theory in which one field supplies both the Galactic boost at R0 and the Solar-System EFE. Hees et al. 2016 had judged the ν̄_α family with α ≥ 2 viable ([MNRAS 455, 449](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.01369)), but Desmond 2024 superseded that verdict by fitting the sharpness of the transition to SPARC directly.

Two caveats limit how final this is. The first is a counter-datum. The MIGHTEE-HI/LADUMA RAR of 130 galaxies prefers a sharp transition, **δ = 4.10 (+1.4/−0.68)**, with a0 = (1.50 ± 0.05)×10⁻¹⁰, which the authors call "consistent with the value required by Solar System gravitational constraints" ([Vărăşteanu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03576), PREPRINT). An earlier, peer-reviewed 19-galaxy MIGHTEE-HI paper points the same way ([MNRAS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20857)). The conflict may therefore be partly a SPARC M/L systematic rather than a law of nature, although Desmond calls the M/L and no-bulge relaxations "ad hoc changes without theoretical justification" ([Desmond 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21638), PREPRINT).

The  second caveat is  that PPN tests  do  not  separate the candidates. The Cassini time delay gives γ − 1 = (2.1 ± 2.3)×10⁻⁵ ([Bertotti, Iess & Tortora 2003](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01997)), and the combined ephemerides give (γ − 1) = (0.55 ± 1.35)×10⁻⁵ and (β − 1) = (−0.45 ± 1.75)×10⁻⁵ ([Fienga & Minazzoli 2024, LRR 27, 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01821)). These bound the Sun's own 1PN field, and every relativistic candidate below can be made GR-like there. When a paper says a theory "passes Solar System tests", it almost always means PPN, not Q2, and the ranking table keeps the two apart.

Wide binaries and the Oort cloud probe the same Galactic-field regime, so in modified gravity (MG) they largely duplicate Cassini. Wide binaries are contested. Chae reports an anomaly of γ_g = 1.37 ([ApJ 972, 186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05720)) and up to 1.61 at the lowest accelerations ([Yoon, Tian & Chae 2025, ApJ 992, 102](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13177)). Banik et al. find α_grav = −0.021 (+0.065/−0.045), which excludes MOND at 16σ ([MNRAS 527, 4573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03436)). Cookson et al. find Newton "up to 1500x more likely" ([MNRAS 547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.24035)), and Saad & Ting's reanalysis of Chae's 36 pairs gives γ = 1.00 (+0.24/−0.19) ([OJAp 9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11015)). The Oort cloud is a separate, robust failure for AQUAL ([Vokrouhlický, Nesvorný & Tremaine 2024, ApJ 968, 47](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09555)), though that paper explicitly does "not rule out some MOND theories more elaborate than AQUAL, in which non-Newtonian effects are screened".

## Escapes where Q2 stops being a MOND observable, and what each one costs

**Modified inertia** is the cleanest escape, and Park et al. name it as one: "Another viable possibility is for MOND to be a modification of inertia." Hees et al. 2014 had already stated that the quadrupole "is not present in the Modified Inertia approach", asserting it with a citation to [Milgrom 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.1611) rather than deriving it. The derivation is in Milgrom's preprint [arXiv:2310.14334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14334) (PREPRINT). Inside his models, a planet that always feels g ≫ a0 has no anomaly beyond 1 − μ(g⊙/a0), and with the RAR form ν(y) = 1/(1 − e^{−√y}) the Earth's correction is ν − 1 ≈ e^{−10⁴}. In plain terms, MI leaves the inner Solar System Newtonian unless its interpolating function's high-acceleration tail is slow. The peer-reviewed core is [Milgrom 1994, Ann. Phys. 229, 384](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9303012), which proves that *any* MI theory gives the exact algebraic RAR on circular orbits and that Galilei-invariant MI must be strongly nonlocal, and [Milgrom 2022, PRD 106, 064060](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07073), which gives the current models with an EFE set by μ(θ⟨a_ex⟩/a0), θ(0) > 1.

MI pays in two ways. **No action-based relativistic MI exists**, so there are no lensing, cosmology or gravitational-wave predictions, and causality and well-posed initial values are unresolved. The galaxy data also push back. Chae finds "a 6.9σ difference between the inner and outer parts on an acceleration plane which would be inconsistent with current proposals of modified inertia" ([ApJ 941, 55](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11069)), to which Milgrom replies that the analysis used the exact circular relation and so "did not test MI". Petersen & Lelli find MG and MI both within 1.5σ ([A&A 636, A56](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03348)), and FIRE-2 RAR "hooks" are "challenging to explain" in MI ([Mercado et al. 2024, MNRAS 530, 1349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09507)).

For  this program, the  key point is  that  **the  SPARC fits carry over to MI unchanged for circular orbits**. The tests then move to two places. The first is the tail of μ at x ~ 10⁵–10⁸. The simple ν_1 family leaves a constant anomaly of about a0 toward the Sun, which ephemerides exclude in MI as well as in MG, while exponential-tail families pass trivially (the researcher's arithmetic gives 1 − μ ~ e^{−700} at Saturn). The analytic precession formulae for power-law and exponential tails in [Chan & Lee 2023, MNRAS 518, 6238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01019) are the tool for checking the program's ν_n tails. The second is the MI wide-binary EFE. The researcher's arithmetic gives velocity boosts of 1.12 for θ(0) = 2 and 1.08 for θ(0) = 3, against 1.22 in MG, so MI sits closer to the null wide-binary results than QUMOND does. Matching Banik et al. would still need θ(0) ≳ 4–6; that is an inference, since no published MI fit to wide binaries exists. Milgrom's **mixed MG–MI family** ([arXiv:2610.21699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21699), PREPRINT, 15 Oct 2026) adds a dial 0 ≤ η ≤ 1 between the two theories, with *different* IFs in the kinetic and field equations. Structurally, that is exactly what the program's null result calls for.

**Superfluid dark matter** passes Cassini by assertion. Berezhiani & Khoury estimate that the Sun's phonon gradient exceeds the critical velocity within r ≲ 250 (m/eV)^{−1/3}(Λ/meV)^{−5/9} AU, so "the usual worries about MOND and local tests of gravity do not apply" ([PRD 92, 103510](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.01019)), and the fiducial model lists "Solar system: Newtonian" ([Berezhiani, Famaey & Khoury, JCAP 09 (2018) 021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05748)). That estimate is a scaling argument: no paper models the normal-phase "bubble" or computes Q2. Mistele states that extending the galaxy-scale phenomenology naively gives Solar-System accelerations "much too large", and that the high-acceleration regime is not understood ([JCAP 01 (2021) 025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.03003)). The galaxy record counts against SfDM. Its SPARC fits show "an unnatural dependence on the size of the galaxy" and often fall outside the MOND regime ([A&A 664, A40](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07282)), and on weak lensing "the SFDM model is incompatible with the weak-lensing observations, at least in its current form" ([JCAP 09 (2023) 004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08560)). Because the bubble decouples the planets from the Galactic phonon force, SfDM is the one hybrid that escapes Park et al.'s Milky Way 2% corollary, but only if the bubble picture is right.

Two  further escapes give  up most of  what  made  MOND attractive. **Baryon-interacting DM** has "no EFE" at all, so Q2 ≈ 0 ([Famaey, Khoury & Penco, JCAP 03 (2018) 038](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01316)). It therefore cannot explain the EFE detections, which are >4σ statistically and 8σ and 11σ in NGC 5033 and NGC 5055 ([Chae et al. 2020, ApJ 904, 51](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11525)). **MOG** has a linear, Newton-plus-Yukawa weak field, so only the ordinary Galactic tide of about 10⁻³⁰ s⁻² enters Q2 (the researcher's inference) and it passes trivially. Its SPARC RAR, however, needs α and μ that "run with galaxy mass" ([Green & Moffat 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09476)), and with parameters fixed by THINGS it "is ruled out at 5.49σ" by Dragonfly 44 ([Haghi et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07978)).

## The open niche: non-universal interpolation where Q2 was never computed

Desmond, Hees & Famaey state the loophole directly: TRIMOND and GQUMOND "may evade our constraints by having different IFs in galaxies, wide binaries and the SS" ([arXiv:2401.04796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04796)). Park et al. conclude that "any MOND-based modified gravity must involve another scale beyond a0 … or a screening mechanism". A sweep of the INSPIRE citation lists of Park 2026, Desmond 2024 and Mistele 2024 found **no native Q2 computation** for two-field TeVeS or AeST, AeST at finite m_×, TRIMOND, extended BIMOND, khronon, generalized Einstein-aether, nonlocal MOND or any screened MOND. The researchers judged this "a real open niche, not a search failure", with the caveat that the full texts of the citing papers were not searched.

**GQUMOND** ([Milgrom 2023, PRD 108, 084005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01589)) is the most direct match for the program's result. Its Lagrangian depends on higher derivatives of the Newtonian potential through a length ℓ0 (variant A) or a frequency Ω0 (variant B, f = f[Ω0⁻⁴ψ,ijψ,ij]). It reduces to QUMOND on galactic scales by construction, so **the SPARC side of the program's scans transfers verbatim**. Near the phantom-mass region the Sun's own field sets scales of "∼6000 au, and a timescale of ∼4.5×10⁵ years", and if those are below ℓ0 or Ω0⁻¹ "the MOND anomaly is greatly reduced". No Q2 number, fitted ℓ0 or SPARC fit has been published. Two points follow as inferences rather than literature claims. The program's tidal-coordinate screen, which is QUMOND screened on ‖∇∇Φ_N‖ according to the program's own records, is already a GQUMOND variant-B construction, so GQUMOND gives it a peer-reviewed parent. Those same records show that, at its pre-registered Lagrangian zero C = 0, that screen failed the Oort cloud and is tiered [X]. The working C that passes is a further constant tuned to the solar neighbourhood and carried as a declared input. Separately, any screen that kills MOND up to wide-binary scales must also face the preprint claim that Gaia open clusters sit on the RAR ([Huisjes & Hernandez 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03522), PREPRINT).

**AeST**  ([Skordis & Zlosnik  2021, PRL 127,  161302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00082))  matters  most for the program's covariant completion. [Mistele 2024, PRD 110, 024062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07742) shows that its quasi-static limit depends on m_× = sqrt[(2−K_B)/(2K_B)]·Q0. As m_× → ∞, it is single-field AQUAL plus a ghost-condensate density. As m_× → 0, it is a *two-field* MOND: a Newtonian piece Φ − α combined with an AQUAL-type scalar α that has its own function μ̃. This is the TeVeS-like structure of a Newtonian potential plus an AQUAL scalar, not QUMOND's two-field reading, so it does not by itself supply the second field that the program's records say the tidal screen needs. The two limits agree wherever curl terms vanish, as in spherical symmetry. That is why rotation curves barely depend on m_× while external-field configurations do.

The reduction to AQUAL for m_× r_M ≫ 1 is computed. The claim that realistic m_× is of order the ghost-condensate scale, about 1 Mpc⁻¹, is an order-of-magnitude assertion. On that estimate, the two-field limit "is relevant for wide binaries (l ∼ kAU), and at least the inner parts of galaxies", which **would place the Sun's EFE region at r_M ≈ 8 kAU in the two-field regime, not the AQUAL one**. Park et al., whose co-authors include the AeST author Durakovic, say only that covariant theories "mostly" reduce to AQUAL/QUMOND. The program's current-state record carries "AeST reduces to AQUAL"; Mistele's result undercuts that, conditional on his estimate of m_×. In the two-field limit, Q2 comes from α alone, with a boundary gradient set by the Galactic *α*-field, which is smaller than the total field. The IF→RAR and IF→Q2 maps therefore differ, but whether Q2 is suppressed enough has not been computed, and Mistele's percent-level wide-binary result suggests a change rather than elimination (inference). Against AeST, it deviates from MOND at weak-lensing radii where the data "show no clear indication of these predicted deviations" ([A&A 676, A100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03499)). No PPN or preferred-frame α1/α2 computation exists either, and the PRL itself notes that its screening branch "may be in conflict with Mercury's orbit".

**TRIMOND** ([Milgrom 2023, PRD 108, 063009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19986)) gives a system-independent RAR for any one-dimensional mass distribution, while "their variety appears in nonsymmetric situations". The paper names the Galactic field's effect on the Solar System as one such situation, and Milgrom suggests, without derivation, that TRIMOND could be the non-relativistic limit of BIMOND with an added scalar. Standard BIMOND itself reduces exactly to QUMOND ([PRD 80, 123536](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.0790)), and extended BIMOND collapses to a single function in the same limit ([PRD 106, 084010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10882)), so neither of those escapes.

**Galileon  k-mouflage** ([Babichev, Deffayet  & Esposito-Farèse 2011,  PRD  84,  061502(R)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.2538)) is the only screened MOND with computed Solar-System numbers. Its scalar force falls to about (r/22 au)⁴ of the PN force, with α1 ≈ 2×10⁻¹², but those numbers are for an isolated Sun, with no external field and no SPARC fit. The researcher's arithmetic puts the residual linear force at κ ≈ 3.6×10⁻²⁷ s⁻². That is a monopole rather than the quadrupole, but it is comparable to Cassini's sensitivity and needs an ephemeris refit. The cosmology of g-TeVeS fails ([Złośnik & Skordis 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00683)). The program's own records add that the retracted in-house Vainshtein claim should not be revived from this literature, and that the required exponent p = 1.890 is not supplied by a cubic Galileon.

**Khronon** gives a "modified Poisson-type potential" for stationary systems ([Blanchet & Marsat 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.5264)) and "the same PPN limit as GR" ([Blanchet & Skordis, JCAP 11 (2024) 040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06584)), and Bonetti & Barausse computed a viable PPN and pulsar region ([PRD 91, 084053](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05554)). The quasi-stationary Sun–Galaxy system most likely inherits the AQUAL Q2 (inference), because Flanagan's order-unity correction applies only to non-stationary systems ([ApJ 958, 107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.14846)). **Nonlocal MOND** ([Deffayet, Esposito-Farèse & Woodard 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4984)) is the one family whose EFE is *undefined*. Its "exponentially small" Solar-System deviations cover only the isolated Sun, and the authors still list the EFE as future work, describing the open question as "how distant masses affect the nonlocal functional" ([Deffayet & Woodard, JCAP 04 (2026) 081](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10513)).

## Conclusion

The  program's null result  matches the field's  own  diagnosis:  a single-function Poisson MOND cannot fit SPARC and Cassini together, and the published exits are a new scale, screening, or modified inertia. None of these exits has yet been carried through to a Q2 number plus a SPARC fit. Every candidate that truly separates Solar-System behaviour from galaxy behaviour is uncomputed at exactly the decisive step, which makes the gap an opportunity rather than a dead end. It also means a "pass" announced from PPN alone, or from an isolated-Sun calculation, proves nothing about Q2.

The  ranking and the  priorities answer different  questions.  GQUMOND  ranks first as raw material because its SPARC side transfers unchanged and it gives the program's tidal screen a peer-reviewed parent, though that screen's Oort-cloud result means the parentage settles nothing about viability. The calculation with the most leverage is **Q2 in AeST's two-field regime**, because it tests the covariant completion the program already uses. If Mistele's m_× estimate holds, the program's AeST = AQUAL assumption is wrong for the Sun. The calculation is a scalar AQUAL problem of Blanchet–Novak cost, and its outcome decides whether that completion has an escape built in. A modified-inertia reading is the inexpensive cross-check: re-score the existing SPARC fits unchanged and test only their high-acceleration tails against the ephemerides.
