# Bullet offset equation

The  identification  of the  lensing  peak with  the  collisionless  galaxies is  `[O]`. The integral below is `[D]` once that identification is granted. No action was varied.

Let  x_★  be the  position  of the  collisionless  component  and x_g  the position of the gas peak. The lensing peak is taken to ride with the galaxies,

x_ℓ(t) = x_★(t).

After  core  passage at  time  t_c the  two  velocities  are not  the same, because the gas is slowed and the galaxies are not:

dΔ/dt = v_★ − v_g,    Δ ≡ x_★ − x_g.

So the offset at a later time is

Δ(t) = ∫_{t_c}^{t} (v_★ − v_g) dt′.

If  those  two speeds  are  steady after  core  passage,  the integral  collapses to

Δ = (v_★ − v_g) (t − t_c).

The  X-ray  shock speed  is  not v_g.  The  shock  outruns the  gas bullet, so substituting the shock speed, or the shock speed minus a halo speed, is a different equation. This one has two inputs it does not contain: the galaxy–gas speed difference, and a time since core passage that was not fit to the lensing map.

## Convergence

The  convergence  is a  functional  of the  two  baryon  densities, not  of the two peak coordinates. Light follows the same potential that the divergence equation solves, so the convergence is half the sky-plane Laplacian of that potential. The Newtonian potential inside it is sourced by the gas plus the galaxies. The two-blob run of this functional peaks on the gas.

∇² Φ_N = 4πG (ρ_★ + ρ_g + ρ_×)

∇² ψ = ∇ · (ν_eff ∇ Φ_N)

κ = (1/2) ∇_⊥² ψ

The  first  line is  the  baryon source  plus  ρ_×.  The second  is the surviving disk equation. The third reads the lensing convergence off that potential. The galaxies do not outweigh the gas. The added density has to, and it has to travel with them. The role is called intension: the job, not the composition. Information tension is the name for why the job exists. Neither name says what the density is made of.
