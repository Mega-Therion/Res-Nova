# Frontier Literature Baseline

This note records external field benchmarks used when designing the Res-Nova research program. It is a planning source, not a claim ledger and not evidence for any Res-Nova result.

## 1. Relativistic MOND reference point

Skordis and Złośnik proposed a relativistic theory designed to reproduce MOND phenomenology while matching the cosmic microwave background and linear matter power spectrum, with a second-order action reported free of ghost instabilities. Res-Nova should treat this as the immediate comparison theory for any Skordis–Złośnik embedding, not as an endorsement or proof of Res-Nova-specific claims.

Source: [Skordis & Złośnik, *New Relativistic Theory for Modified Newtonian Dynamics*, Physical Review Letters 127, 161302 (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.127.161302); [arXiv:2007.00082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00082).

## 2. Time dependence and gravitational-wave sector

Tian et al. examined time evolution of local gravitational parameters and gravitational-wave polarizations in the Skordis–Złośnik theory. Their abstract reports time-varying effective Newtonian and characteristic MOND scales under relaxed static assumptions, and two tensor polarizations propagating at light speed. This makes redshift evolution, local-parameter evolution, and wave-sector consistency mandatory independent workstreams rather than optional appendices.

Source: [Tian et al., *Physical Review D* 107, 044062 (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.044062).

## 3. Program-wide standard for viable modified gravity

A viable modified-gravity program must separate theoretical consistency from observational adequacy. The field tests gravity through solar-system and binary-pulsar constraints, gravitational-wave propagation and strong-field tests, CMB and large-scale structure, galaxies/clusters, and screening signatures. Theoretical alternatives also face constraints from degrees of freedom, stability, and screening mechanisms.

Source: [Ferreira, *Cosmological Tests of Gravity*, Annual Review of Astronomy and Astrophysics 57, 335–374 (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-astro-091918-104423).

## Program implication

Res-Nova should not present a galaxy-scale interpolation function as a completed cosmological replacement. Its frontier claim should instead be narrower: a transparent, falsifiable research program testing whether a specified dual-channel nonrelativistic limit admits a stable covariant completion that survives defined local, wave, lensing, galaxy, and cosmological gates.

## 4. Public empirical inputs

SPARC provides public mass models, photometric data, and high-quality HI+Hα rotation curves for 175 late-type galaxies, with source and citation guidance. It remains a suitable frozen baseline for a galaxy-dynamics replication package, but it cannot by itself distinguish a constant acceleration scale from a horizon-linked redshift evolution.

Source: [SPARC database](https://astroweb.case.edu/SPARC/) and [Case Western Reserve University overview](https://astronomy.case.edu/2016/08/26/sparc-galaxy-database/).

DESI public products provide an independent cosmological input path. The NERSC Cosmology Data Repository describes DESI DR1 spectroscopy as spectra and redshifts for 18 million galaxies, quasars, and stars; DESI’s earlier public release documented the survey-validation data product and its scope. Any Res-Nova cosmology analysis must use a collaboration-approved likelihood/data product, reproduce the cited baseline, and separate exploratory work from pre-registered inference.

Source: [NERSC Cosmology Data Repository](https://portal.nersc.gov/project/cosmo/); [DESI Early Data Release announcement](https://www.desi.lbl.gov/2023/06/13/the-desi-early-data-release-in-now-available/).

## 5. Research-program implication

The first two empirical work packages should therefore be: (a) a clean, checksum-verified SPARC replication with fully declared nuisance treatment and held-out predictive diagnostics; and (b) a separately versioned cosmology branch that begins by reproducing a published reference likelihood before any Res-Nova-specific parameter inference. No cross-scale identification of `a0` should be promoted without a declared forward model, frozen data release, and negative-control analysis.
