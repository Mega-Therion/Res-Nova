## 1. The map

Light from a galaxy reaches us along null geodesics of **one metric**, the same metric gravitational waves follow (C1 below forces this). Each observed quantity is the intrinsic one multiplied by a geometry factor:

| Observed | = intrinsic × geometry | Geometry factor, from the ITT geometry |
|---|---|---|
| Deflection, image positions, arcs | — | α = (2/c²) ∫ ∇⊥Φ_IT dl, where Φ_IT is the potential set by the galaxy-tier ITT law (Block 3, μ_std with measured a₀). In mass language this is the phantom density ρ_ph = [∇·(ν g_N) − ∇·g_N]/4πG |
| Convergence and shear | — | κ = Σ_ph/Σ_cr and γ, the same "dark matter" lensing maps, generated by geometry, not particles |
| Flux | L/(4π D_L²) × μ | magnification μ = 1/[(1−κ)² − γ²] |
| Inferred luminosity and stellar mass | × μ × (D_L,assumed/D_L,true)² | as above |
| Angular size | R/D_A × lensing | D_A = D_L/(1+z)². This duality is exact for any single metric that conserves photons |
| Ages and formation times | t(z) | the expansion history (ΩCDM: Ω_Λ = ln 2, w = −1) |
| Redshift | (1+z_cos)(1+z_grav) | z_grav ≈ Φ_IT/c² ~ 10⁻⁶; negligible |

**What belongs to the dynamics tier, not this map:** internal velocities (rotation curves, dispersions). They are Doppler measurements of motions that the galaxy-tier ITT law sets.

## 2. Hard constraints the map must meet (computed)

**C1. Gravitational waves and light travel together (GW170817).**
- The speed difference between the two is |Δc/c| ≲ 10⁻¹⁵ over 40 Mpc.
- A light-only optical term, such as the corpus's disformal metric g̃ = g + 2ℓ²∇χ∇χ (`ITT_Definitions.lean`), is limited by this to ≲ 7×10⁻¹⁴ inside galaxies. It can deflect light by at most ~7×10⁻¹³ rad.
- Galaxy lensing needs ~10⁻⁵ rad, which is **~1.5×10⁷ times more**.
- **Consequence:** the optical effect that looks like dark matter cannot act on light alone. It has to be the geometry itself (the single metric of the live seat), which light and gravitational waves share. This matches the retirement of disformal photon coupling (`RN-GEA-RETIRED-001`).

**C2. Distance duality, measured (Pantheon+ shape with M free; DESI DR2 BAO with r_d free).**
- Model: D_L = (1+z) D_M (1+z)^ε. Result: **ε = −0.028 ± 0.015** (about 1.9σ from zero; Ω_m = 0.298).
- At face value, distant supernovae look about 7% brighter at z = 2.3 than the BAO geometry predicts (1σ range: 3–11%).
- That is not significant, and it is the known tension between supernovae and BAO over Ω_m.
- A single-metric optical map predicts ε = 0 exactly, so this cannot be the ITT optical effect. Any optical brightening or dimming is bounded at about this level out to z ≈ 2.3.

## 3. What the map does and does not do for JWST

**The live JWST tension.**
- Spectroscopically confirmed galaxies exist out to z ≈ 14.4 (JADES-GS-z14-0, MoM-z14).
- The tension is now mainly the *abundance* of UV-bright galaxies at z > 10, about a factor of a few to ~10 above pre-JWST models.
- It is no longer single galaxies with 50–800× impossible masses: many of the early "too massive" candidates were revised (AGN, wrong redshifts).

**What the optical tier can supply: magnification bias.**
- Lensing by foreground phantom geometry raises the counts of the brightest galaxies, because the bright end of the luminosity function is steep.
- To reproduce galaxy and cluster lensing at all, phantom lensing must match observed lensing strengths. Its magnification statistics are therefore of the same order as the dark-matter-halo case: typically μ ≈ 1–1.5, with a rare strongly lensed tail.
- **It cannot make galaxies systematically 10–100× too bright.**

**The remaining lever is in the dynamics tier.**
- If a₀ tracks the horizon, a₀ ∝ H(z) (O1 test path), then at z ≈ 10 a₀ is ~20× larger. Early galaxies would then sit deeper in the ITT/MOND regime and collapse and form stars earlier.
- That is a candidate explanation for early, bright galaxies at tier 1.
- Its test is O4 (a₀(z) on high-z kinematics), already pre-registered (`PREREG_O4_HIGHZ_A0Z.md`).
