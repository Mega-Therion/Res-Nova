## 1. `[P]` The operator itself is GW170817-safe

In Horndeski the tensor speed is fixed by `G₄` alone: `c_T² = G₄/(G₄ − 2XG_{4X})`. A cubic Galileon is `G₃(φ,X)□φ`, which does not enter `G₄`. With the Einstein–Hilbert normalization `G₄ = M_Pl²/2` (constant), `G_{4X} = 0`, so

```
c_T² = 1   exactly, for any G₃
```

A pure cubic Galileon is safe by construction. Anything touching `G₄` or `G₅` (quartic/quintic) breaks `c_T = 1` and is excluded.

## 2. `[P]` The decisive number: `Q₂` is generated at `y ~ O(1)`, not in the Solar System

Baseline from the validated `efe_quadrupole_q2.py` machinery: `Q₂(μ_std) = 16.66 × 10⁻²⁷ s⁻²`, a **8.37σ** tension against Park et al. `(1.6 ± 1.8)×10⁻²⁷`. Clearing it at 2σ needs a **3.2×** suppression.

So I asked where `Q₂` actually comes from. I imposed a *perfect* screen — `ν_eff(y) = 1` for all `y > y_cut`, i.e. exactly Newtonian above the cut, MOND untouched below — and swept `y_cut`:

| `y_cut` | `Q₂` (×10⁻²⁷) | suppression |
|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 9.82 | 1.70 |
| 3 | 17.41 | 0.96 |
| 10 | 16.70 | 1.00 |
| **30** | **16.662** | **1.00** |
| 10⁴ … 10¹² | 16.662 | 1.00 |

**A perfect Newtonian screen everywhere above `y = 30` changes `Q₂` by 0.031%.** That is the whole result. `Q₂` does not live at Solar-System accelerations.

## 3. `[D]` Why

`y = g/a₀`. The Milky Way's external field at the Sun is `g_e = 1.9×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻²`, so `η = g_e/a₀ = 1.82`, and the quadrupole integrand's weight sits at `y ~ O(η)`. Mercury sits at `y ~ 3×10⁸`. The screen and the signal are eight orders of magnitude apart in acceleration space.

The quadrupole is generated by the **nonlinearity of `ν` near `y ~ 1`**, where the external and internal fields mix. That is precisely the region that produces the SPARC rotation-curve fits.

## 4. `[X]` So the standard route is a no-go

Any mechanism that acts on the acceleration **magnitude** and switches off above some `y_cut` cannot reduce `Q₂`. To move `Q₂` you must change the response at `y ~ O(1)` — and at `y ~ O(1)`, changing the response **is** changing MOND.

This is the same wall as `p ≥ 3/2` from #228, reached from the other side. There, a higher power broke the `1/r` force; here, a screen at the wrong scale cannot reach the observable. Both say: Cassini `Q₂` is not a high-acceleration problem.

## 5. `[O]` Scope — what this does *not* rule out

Precisely: this rules out **isotropic, magnitude-dependent** screening, meaning any `ν_eff(y)` tending to 1 above a threshold.

It does **not** rule out an operator sensitive to the **anisotropy** of `∇ᵢ∇ⱼφ`, which could in principle suppress the `ℓ=2` response while leaving the `ℓ=0` monopole — and hence the rotation curves — intact. That is the one surviving direction.

But: no such operator exists in the corpus, the standard cubic Galileon `(∂φ)²□φ` is not anisotropic, and I have not constructed one. So this is a *named* escape route, not an available one.
