# Transfer-law derivation, source-to-test mapping, and the `ε = 1/2` limit

**Scope:** Gemini memo rim law and the Res Nova dark-bubble WEP audit. The shared memo is external evidence; Res Nova’s repository status remains authoritative for the repository claim labels.

## 1. Transfer equation and wall coordinates

Let the wall occupy

```text
−H/2 ≤ z ≤ +H/2,       H ≡ ΔR,
```

with inner rim at `z_- = −H/2` and outer rim at `z_+ = +H/2`. For a tangential Fourier mode `exp[i(k·x−ωt)]`, the memo’s sub-acoustic radial equation is

```text
c_z² Z''(z) + [ω² − c_parallel² k²] Z(z) = 0.
```

When `ω² < c_parallel² k²`, define

```text
κ² ≡ (c_parallel² k² − ω²)/c_z² > 0,
```

so that

```text
Z'' − κ² Z = 0,
Z(z) = A cosh(κz) + B sinh(κz).
```

In the memo’s incompressible/sub-acoustic limit, `κ = Λ(x)k`. Define

```text
u ≡ κH/2 = (1/2)Λ(x)kΔR.
```

Place the observer at a fractional distance `ε` from the inner rim. Its coordinate is

```text
z_o = −H/2 + εH = (ε − 1/2)H.
```

It is useful to define the centered coordinate

```text
ζ ≡ 2z_o/H = 2ε − 1,
```

so `z_o = ζH/2`.

The distances from the observer to the inner and outer rims are

```text
d_in  = z_o − z_- = εH,
d_out = z_+ − z_o = (1−ε)H.
```

Therefore the corresponding dimensionless decay lengths are

```text
κ d_in  = 2εu,
κ d_out = 2(1−ε)u.
```

This is the first point at which the observer convention enters the transfer law.

## 2. Parity decomposition and the two Jacobians

The centered solution can be written in even and odd pieces,

```text
Z(z_o) = A cosh(ζu) + B sinh(ζu),
```

where `ζu = κz_o`. Rim perturbations excite even/varicose and odd/sinuous branches. The memo’s branch combination can be represented schematically by the two identities

```text
cosh(ζu) cosh(u) + sinh(ζu) sinh(u)
    = cosh[(1+ζ)u],

cosh(ζu) cosh(u) − sinh(ζu) sinh(u)
    = cosh[(1−ζ)u].
```

The common normalization from the two-rim boundary problem is `sinh(2u) = sinh(κH)`. Hence the outer and inner rim responses are

```text
J_out ∝ + cosh[(1+ζ)u] / sinh(2u),
J_in  ∝ − cosh[(1−ζ)u] / sinh(2u).
```

Substituting `ζ = 2ε−1` gives

```text
1+ζ = 2ε,
1−ζ = 2(1−ε),
```

and therefore

```text
J_out ∝ + cosh(2εu) / sinh(2u),
J_in  ∝ − cosh(2(1−ε)u) / sinh(2u).
```

Taking the magnitude ratio produces the exact conditional transfer law

```text
R_rim(u;ε)
  ≡ |J_out/J_in|
  = cosh(2εu) / cosh(2(1−ε)u).
```

This derivation explains the hyperbolic factors. It does **not** select a special numerical value of `ε`.

## 3. Precisely where `√2` appears

If the observer convention is separately chosen as

```text
ε = 1/√2,
```

then

```text
ζ = 2ε−1 = √2−1,
2εu = √2 u,
2(1−ε)u = (2−√2)u.
```

The transfer law becomes

```text
R_rim(u;1/√2)
 = cosh(√2 u) / cosh[(2−√2)u].
```

The distance ratio is

```text
d_in/d_out
 = ε/(1−ε)
 = (1/√2)/(1−1/√2)
 = 1+√2.
```

Thus the `√2` is introduced by the chosen observer geometry and then propagated algebraically through the transfer solution. It is not generated by a new radial eigenvalue, by the AQUAL stiffness tensor, or by solving an additional field equation. The memo’s displayed equations provide no variational or boundary condition that dynamically derives `ε = 1/√2`; it is an input to the geometric evaluation.

The asymptotic forms follow directly. For `u ≪ 1`,

```text
cosh(au) = 1 + a²u²/2 + O(u⁴),

R_rim = 1 + 2(2ε−1)u² + O(u⁴).
```

At `ε = 1/√2`,

```text
R_rim = 1 + 2(√2−1)u² + O(u⁴).
```

For `u ≫ 1`, `cosh(au) ~ exp(au)/2`, so

```text
R_rim ~ exp{2(2ε−1)u}.
```

At `ε = 1/√2` and `2u = ΛkΔR`,

```text
R_rim ~ exp[(√2−1)Λ(x)kΔR].
```

## 4. What must be mapped from a source to a test body

To compare LLR and MICROSCOPE correctly, the model needs two response coefficients for every macroscopic body `A`:

- `κ_A^A`: active/source response, which controls the field generated by `A`;
- `κ_A^P`: passive/test response, which controls how `A` accelerates in an external field.

Let `E_a^A` be the inertial energy in sector `a` of body `A`, and let `w_a^A` be the model’s gravitational weight for that sector. Define

```text
M_A^I = (1/c²) Σ_a E_a^A,
M_A^A = (1/c²) Σ_a w_a^A E_a^A,
κ_A^A = M_A^A/M_A^I.
```

For a test-body coupling that is not assumed identical to the source coupling, define analogously

```text
M_A^P = (1/c²) Σ_a w_{a,P}^A E_a^A,
κ_A^P = M_A^P/M_A^I.
```

Minimal single-metric coupling corresponds to `w_a = 1` for every sector, so `κ_A^A = κ_A^P = 1`. The dark-bubble B/C rule instead assigns different weights to sectors. In the Res Nova audit, the leading non-Abelian gluon channel has weight `−1`, while the Abelian Coulomb channel has weight `+1`; the resulting composition dependence is what must be propagated into macroscopic bodies.

For a source `S` and test body `T` separated by `r`, the leading Newtonian acceleration should be written as

```text
a_T←S = −G_* M_S^I κ_S^A κ_T^P / r²,
```

before calibration. A measured laboratory or ephemeris constant absorbs a reference product, for example

```text
G_ref = G_* κ_{S0}^A κ_{T0}^P.
```

This calibration step matters: a universal rescaling of every body can be absorbed into `G_ref`; only composition-dependent differences survive as WEP observables.

## 5. MICROSCOPE prediction from the mapping

MICROSCOPE has one external source to leading order: Earth. Its two concentric test masses have compositions Ti and Pt. The Earth factor is common to both accelerations:

```text
a_Ti = −G_* M_E^I κ_E^A κ_Ti^P/r²,

a_Pt = −G_* M_E^I κ_E^A κ_Pt^P/r².
```

The Eötvös ratio is

```text
η_MIC
 = 2(a_Ti−a_Pt)/(a_Ti+a_Pt)
 = 2(κ_Ti^P−κ_Pt^P)/(κ_Ti^P+κ_Pt^P)
 ≈ κ_Ti^P−κ_Pt^P,
```

where the final approximation holds for small deviations around one. The active Earth factor cancels at this order. A proton-level `M_p^A/M_p^I` therefore cannot be compared directly with the MICROSCOPE bound unless the model derives how that proton source anomaly changes `κ_Ti^P−κ_Pt^P`, including the composition of the test masses and their binding energies.

The correct MICROSCOPE gate would compute sector fractions for Ti and Pt, form their passive coefficients, and predict `η_MIC` with the same composition convention used by the experiment. If the model imposes `w_{a,P}=w_a`, then the passive Ti/Pt difference is inherited from the sector weights. If it only changes source `T^{00}` while keeping brane test bodies minimally coupled to the induced metric, then `κ_Ti^P=κ_Pt^P=1` and the model has no direct MICROSCOPE signal at this order, even though it may have an active-source problem.

## 6. LLR prediction and the active channel

LLR is not represented by simply substituting the proton ratio into a MICROSCOPE bound. A consistent LLR calculation must use an ephemeris-level force model containing Earth, Moon, Sun, and relevant spacecraft/planetary bodies. For each ordered pair,

```text

```

plus the model’s self-energy and finite-size terms if present.

At the simplest point-mass level, the Sun-induced Earth–Moon differential acceleration is

```text
Δa_EM←S ∝ κ_S^A(κ_E^P−κ_M^P).
```

This makes an important limitation visible: an **active** Earth/Moon composition difference does not automatically appear in the leading Sun-directed differential acceleration. It affects the gravitational fields generated by Earth and Moon and therefore their mutual dynamics, fitted masses, and the full ephemeris residuals. The active channel must be propagated through the complete N-body model rather than relabelled as passive WEP.

## 7. Consequences of exact unity at `ε = 1/2`

```text

ζ = 0,
J_out ∝ cosh(u)/sinh(2u),
J_in  ∝ −cosh(u)/sinh(2u),
R_rim = 1.
```

The observer sees equal-magnitude sensitivity to both rims with opposite parity sign. In the centered solution,

```text
Z(0) = A,
```

because `sinh(0)=0`; the sinuous/odd component vanishes at the midpoint. Hence the wall equation still has its evanescent modes, finite-depth scale, and AQUAL anisotropic decay rate, but it does not produce an outer-rim dominance observable for that observer.

Several claims must consequently be removed or scoped if `ε = 1/2` is retained:

- the quoted exponential outer-rim enhancement;
- the numerical sequence `1.0021` through `62.76`;
- the statement that inner-rim perturbations are exponentially hidden for the canonical observer;
- any inference that the wall equation itself selects an outer rim.

Asymmetry could still arise from unequal physical boundary conditions, unequal rim impedances, a non-midplane observer, background gradients that break radial reflection symmetry, or nonlinear coupling. But it would no longer come from the symmetric transfer problem evaluated at its midpoint. The memo must therefore choose between a symmetric midplane result and the off-center `ε = 1/√2` result before assigning a single physical interpretation.
