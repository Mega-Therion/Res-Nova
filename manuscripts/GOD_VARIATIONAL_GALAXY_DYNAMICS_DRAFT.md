# Geometrically Ordered Dynamics: Variational Structure, Galaxy-Dynamics Benchmarks, and Relativistic Consistency Tests

**Working manuscript — not yet submission-ready**  
**Status:** Draft framework; numerical and theoretical claims below are explicitly qualified.  
**Authors:** Ryan Yett and collaborators (author list and affiliations to be confirmed)

## Abstract

We examine Geometrically Ordered Dynamics (G.O.D.) / Res Nova as a proposed framework for modified galactic dynamics, focusing on a constitutive interpolation law, its variational primitive, galaxy-rotation-curve benchmarks, and relativistic consistency constraints. The current evidence establishes a mathematical primitive for the chosen standard interpolation function, but does not establish a unique, fully consistent covariant completion or a unified theory of gravity. An important benchmark correction is necessary: the two canonical SPARC comparison rows currently reported are both \(\mu_{\rm std}(x)=x/\sqrt{1+x^2}\) models with different acceleration scales, not separate MOND and G.O.D. implementations. At Tier 0, the fitted \(a_0\) row has the lower median \(\chi^2_\nu\) (9.93 versus 11.08); at Tier 1, the two rows are near-tied (3.41 versus 3.36). The NFW comparison has a lower Tier-1 median (1.92) but uses 716 parameters rather than 374. These results do not demonstrate that G.O.D. outperforms MOND. We separate analytic derivations, computational measurements, conjectures, and failed branches. The paper's purpose is to make the current result auditable and falsifiable, not to claim more than the evidence supports. Numerical claims remain conditional on reproducing the canonical pipeline and verifying its generated outputs.

**Keywords:** modified gravity; galactic dynamics; MOND; variational methods; SPARC; covariant field theory; reproducibility

## 1. Scope and epistemic standard

This manuscript evaluates a specific, versioned set of G.O.D./Res Nova claims. It does not treat the entire Res Nova research corpus as one established theory. Claims are assigned one of the following statuses:

- **[P] Proven:** a named formal theorem proves the stated mathematical proposition, and the theorem's assumptions match the claim.
- **[D] Derived:** follows analytically from stated assumptions, with derivation available for review.
- **[C] Computed:** numerical or empirical result generated by a specified pipeline; reproducibility and uncertainty must be reported.
- **[O] Open:** conjectural, underived, or not yet decisively tested.
- **[X] Excluded:** fails a declared criterion or preregistered test in the stated model branch.

A Lean proof of an algebraic statement is not by itself evidence that the statement is the correct physical model. A numerical fit is not a derivation of the fitted law. A test passed by a quasistatic surrogate is not a test passed by an uncompleted covariant theory. Branches, conventions, and parameters must not be mixed to make a combined claim.

## 2. Constitutive law and variational primitive

The current canonical interpolation function is

\[
\mu_{\rm std}(x)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}},\qquad x\geq 0.
\]

A corresponding dimensionless primitive, normalized to vanish at zero, is

\[
F_{\rm std}(x)=\frac12\left[x\sqrt{1+x^2}-\operatorname{arsinh}(x)\right].
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
F'_{\rm std}(x)=\frac{x^2}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}=x\mu_{\rm std}(x).
\]

This identity establishes the variational primitive for the stated constitutive function. It does not, without the complete action and its metric and scalar variations, establish a relativistic theory. The exact conventions, dimensions, coupling constants, boundary terms, and normalization used in the canonical action must be included in the final version.

### 2.1 Convex-duality identity

For the conjugate variable \(p=F'_{\rm std}(x)=x^2/\sqrt{1+x^2}\), the nonnegative inverse is

\[
x(p)=\sqrt{\frac{p^2+p\sqrt{p^2+4}}{2}}.
\]

The Legendre-Fenchel transform on this monotone branch is

\[
F^*_{\rm std}(p(x))=xp(x)-F_{\rm std}(x)
=\frac12\left[\frac{x(x^2-1)}{\sqrt{1+x^2}}+\operatorname{arsinh}(x)\right].
\]

These expressions should be retained as a mathematical appendix result and checked against the canonical symbolic/numerical test before publication. They must not be presented as evidence of a unique physical Hamiltonian or a completed field-theoretic duality.

## 3. Galaxy-dynamics benchmark

The canonical benchmark must use the current \(\mu_{\rm std}\) branch, pinned inputs, explicit data manifest, and matched evaluation rules across models. The current repository record reports the following benchmark summaries:

| Benchmark surface | MOND baseline | Current G.O.D. implementation | Current interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Tier 0 | 9.93 | 11.08 | MOND baseline currently scores better |
| Tier 1 | 3.36 | 3.41 | Near tie; MOND slightly better |
| Aggregate | 6.09 | 6.03 | Small aggregate difference; definition and aggregation must be documented |

**These values are provisional manuscript inputs, not independently reproduced results in this drafting pass.** The final paper must state the metric represented by each number, the exact sample and masking rules, whether lower is better, all parameter counts, the objective function, and the aggregation formula. The record specifies Tier 0 as using mass-to-light ratios at prior means, and reports a 374-parameter budget comprising 171 disk mass-to-light ratios, 32 bulge mass-to-light ratios, and 171 gas-distance factors. These definitions must be cross-checked against generated tables and the canonical parameter ledger before release.

The earlier \(\mu_{\rm dual}\)-era values are superseded and must not be presented as current evidence. A clean-environment run should reproduce the generated tables from the pinned raw-data manifest and verify that the output agrees with the parameter ledger.

### 3.1 Required comparisons and reporting

The final reproducibility package must report:

1. SPARC data version, source, licence, and SHA-256 manifest.
2. Galaxy inclusion/exclusion masks and treatment of distance and inclination uncertainties.
3. Exact MOND and G.O.D. equations, parameter bounds, priors, and fit procedure.
4. NFW comparison on a clearly defined and matched protocol.
5. Number of fitted parameters for each tier and model, with shared versus galaxy-specific parameters distinguished.
6. Per-galaxy residuals, aggregate metric definition, uncertainty intervals, and sensitivity checks.
7. Exact commands, dependency versions, commit SHA, exit status, and machine-readable outputs.

No statement of superiority should be made unless the comparison is statistically and methodologically justified. A near tie in one aggregate is not evidence of a new physical law.

## 4. Relativistic completion and external-field constraints

A central open problem is whether the quasistatic constitutive law can be embedded in a single, dimensionally consistent covariant action that yields the claimed scalar and metric field equations. The manuscript must identify one canonical branch and derive all relevant equations from that action, including boundary terms and stress-energy conservation.

The current research record distinguishes a scalar-channel function from the total acceleration law in the D7/D9 AeST-related branches. The spherical correspondence record reports that the function reproducing the fitted total law is not identical, in general, to the function that places \(\mu_{\rm std}\) in the scalar channel. The discrepancy can be large for some coupling values. This is not a notational detail: the selected function and coupling must be fixed before Solar-System limits are interpreted.

A quasistatic QUMOND-level Cassini calculation is not equivalent to a Cassini pass by the full covariant theory. The final theory must be tested on the same branch for:

- Cassini time-delay and relevant planetary-precession constraints;
- luminal or otherwise observationally permitted tensor propagation, including GW170817;
- conservation and universal free fall;
- absence of ghosts and gradient instabilities;
- well-posed field equations and acceptable characteristic speeds;
- nonspherical and external-field configurations, not only spherical anchors.

The current record identifies C2 spherical-anchor and C3 axisymmetric/nonspherical own-theory solves as outstanding gates. Until those are completed and independently reproduced, the covariant completion remains **[O]**.

## 5. Screening branches: failures and open routes

Screening mechanisms must be assessed as separate, versioned theories. Results from distinct branches cannot be combined into one successful model unless a single action derives the combined mechanism and all tests are rerun.

The current record marks the preregistered tidal-screen branch with \(C=0\) as **[X]** because its Oort-cloud calculation produces a radial-gravity sign change and a comet-binding distribution inconsistent with the stated target. A later working value of \(C\) improves several reported comparisons, but \(C\) is an additional tuned input fixed to the Solar-neighbourhood target and is not derived from the action. That branch remains provisional and must be reported with its parameter budget, preregistration history, and all independent tests.

The one-field environmental variable \(\chi\) remains open in the canonical record because it does not simultaneously supply the environmental values needed at the Sun and in the Milky Way under the tested construction. The \(\nu_6\) route and other candidate screens must be evaluated by their own full-action derivations and C2/C3 outputs. A passing surrogate calculation must be labelled as such.

## 6. Cosmological and cross-domain claims

High-redshift acceleration trends, holographic dark-energy variants, the \(\ln 2\) coefficient, quantum or warp-drive proposals, and the ITT curvature claim are outside the core result of this paper unless they can be connected to the same canonical action through explicit derivations and independent evidence.

The current research record reports that one late-time HDE edge does not survive the fixed CMB/BAO comparison against \(\Lambda\)CDM, and that the \(\ln 2\) coefficient remains open. Those results should not be presented as successful predictions. The warp-drive construction invokes a separate postulated massive scalar field whose existence is not derived by the galaxy-dynamics action. The ITT curvature claim requires its own action, normalization, variation, conservation, and weak-field derivation. These topics should be reserved for separate papers unless the unified-action gate is actually passed.

## 7. Falsifiability and decisive tests

The final paper must identify prospective tests that could distinguish the canonical model from matched baselines, and state in advance which result would reject it. Candidate tests recorded in the research programme include mass-stratified wide-binary signals in future Gaia data, Oort-cloud dynamics, and the C2/C3 own-theory external-field solutions. These are not all completed or decisive at present. Each test needs a preregistered statistic, selection criteria, uncertainty model, and a clear comparison against Newtonian, MOND, and relevant alternative models.

No post-hoc parameter change may be described as a prediction. If a parameter is fixed using one system, the model must predict the others without retuning; otherwise it must be counted as an additional fit parameter and the corresponding claim downgraded.

## 8. Limitations

At the current evidence state, this programme has not established a complete, unique covariant theory of gravity or a Theory of Everything. The interpolation-function identities are mathematical results; the SPARC results are computational comparisons; the covariant completion and several screening choices remain open; at least one preregistered screening branch has failed a stated test. These distinctions are central to the scientific value of the work and should remain visible in the abstract, tables, and conclusions.

## 9. Conclusion

G.O.D./Res Nova provides a structured programme for investigating a constitutive law, its variational formulation, and its consequences for galaxy dynamics. The current results justify continued, reproducible investigation, but they do not yet justify a claim of full unification. The immediate scientific task is to reproduce the current \(\mu_{\rm std}\) benchmark, freeze and vary one canonical action, resolve the scalar-channel/total-law discrepancy, and complete the C2/C3 and relativistic consistency gates. The publication should report the resulting branch verdict plainly, including failures and remaining open questions. A narrower, reproducible result is preferable to an unsupported unification claim.
