## Record 1
Title: A tidal screen with one constant fixed by the Oort cloud
Files: working_c_normal.pdf, working_c_normal.tex, working_c_body.tex, working_c_alien.pdf, working_c_alien.tex, alien_preamble.tex
Abstract: A tidal screen adds one constant, C, to a two-field Newtonian theory. The constant drops out of the rotation-curve law and remains in the shell stress. At C=0 the shell pushes new comets below the observed Oort peak. The value used here is the centre of the window that puts that peak back, C=0.579125 at a Galactic field 1.9e-10 m/s^2. At that value the Cassini quadrupole is unchanged, a clean Gaia wide-binary cut prefers the model to Newton, 171 SPARC galaxies are unchanged at the level of the fit, and four dwarfs take stellar mass-to-light ratios. The same clean cut was repeated at the two edges of the window. All three pass. A radial integration of comet orbits agrees with the energy proxy to 1.3e-6 au^-1. The non-relativistic action that produces the field equation is written down. The constant C is not fixed by that action. A relativistic completion in which the tide invariant is dynamical, a full comet population in the sense of Vokrouhlicky, Nesvorny and Tremaine, and a cosmological sector are not in this paper.

## Record 2
Title: The Oort cloud rules out the tidal screen at C = 0
Files: oort_c0_normal.pdf, oort_c0_normal.tex, oort_c0_body.tex, oort_c0_alien.pdf, oort_c0_alien.tex, alien_preamble.tex
Abstract: The tidal screen of the companion paper, with the Lagrangian constant set to zero, puts new comets on the wrong side of the observed Oort peak. A comet that falls in from rest arrives with a Newtonian 1/a of about 1.23e-4 au^-1, so its original semimajor axis is at most about 8100 au. The observed peak of new comets lies between 1e4 and 1e5 au. The push sits in a shell near 9 to 12 thousand au, where the net gravity on a test body points outward. Three evaluations of the same map agree at the percent level. A radial orbit integration at the same C=0 lands the same way. This paper records that null. It does not introduce a new constant.
